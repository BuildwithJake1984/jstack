# Getting Started with J-Stack

Welcome to J-Stack, a free collection of workflows, resources, and ideas to help you bring AI into your life and business. I’ll help you find what’s relevant and walk you through where to start.

## Start J-Stack

You are in the right place. Start by reading this guide, then tell your assistant what you would like help with. You can also browse the [catalog](README.md) yourself without connecting any accounts.

J-Stack is a **read-only guide**, not an automation engine. Opening a link does not install anything, run a skill or script, connect or sync an account, send a message, or schedule a check-in. Reading this repository never grants permission to act. Any later action needs your instruction and the permissions required by the app or assistant you are using.

There is no required plugin or marketplace installation. Support for particular hosts, plugins, or Muse is not promised by this guide.

## 1. Plan your optional account connections

Email, calendar, and Drive can provide useful context, but they are not required. Start with one outcome you care about. If your assistant does not already have relevant, authorized context, it should ask: **“What would you like AI to help you with in your life or business?”** A short answer is enough; you can describe a project without sharing private records.

If a connection would help that outcome, use your host app’s connection or integration settings. Available connections and permission choices vary. Never assume that you have a Gmail account, a Google Calendar, Drive, or an available connector.

| Connection | Why you might use it | Choose the account and access deliberately |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gmail or another email service | Understand a task you select, find relevant correspondence, or prepare a reply for review. | Confirm the intended personal or work mailbox. Review whether requested access includes reading, sending, or modifying messages; a reading task does not authorize sending or changes. |
| Calendar | Understand availability or the timing of a project you want help with. | Confirm the account and calendars that matter. Review read versus edit permissions. Finding a time does not authorize creating invitations or changing events. |
| Google Drive or another document service | Find and read the specific project documents you choose. | Confirm the account, files, or folders in scope. Prefer narrower access where available. Reading a document does not authorize editing, deleting, or sharing it. |

Before connecting:

1. Check that the host offers the connection you want. If it does not, skip it and provide a brief description or an appropriate excerpt instead.
2. Confirm the signed-in account. Do not mix a personal account with a work account unintentionally, and follow any organization requirements.
3. Read the permission screen and choose the least access that supports your goal where the service allows that choice. If the available access feels too broad, decline it; J-Stack still works as a catalog.
4. Approve only the connection you intend. Do not share passwords, API keys, recovery codes, or other secrets in chat or in this repository.
5. Ask the host to confirm what is actually available. A connected service is not permission to inspect everything in it; identify the relevant task and material first.

You can disconnect later in the host’s connection settings and, where applicable, revoke the host’s access in the provider’s account settings. Check the host and provider’s current instructions for the exact steps. Disconnecting is separate from deleting previously saved information; review those controls separately if needed.

Your assistant should not connect accounts automatically, request every service “just in case,” or claim access it does not have. It should use only context that is actually available and authorized for this task, and ask you when the goal or scope is unclear.

## 2. Use the guidance responsibly

J-Stack is free educational material. Treat it as a starting point for your own checks, not a guarantee that a tool, workflow, or suggestion is right for you.

- Verify important details against current primary sources, especially prices, permissions, product features, and consequential decisions.
- Use your judgment and get qualified advice when a decision calls for it. You decide which guidance to follow and remain responsible for your choices.
- Review third-party terms, licenses, security, and costs before using a resource. “Free J-Stack” does not mean every linked product is free.
- Keep private information and secrets out of public questions, issues, pull requests, and examples.
- Review a proposed action before authorizing it. Permission to read, permission to act, and permission to repeat an action are different choices.

### Terms review and separate consent

[J-Stack’s Terms of Use](TERMS.md) are currently **DRAFT FOR LEGAL REVIEW**. This PR previews the consent flow; it does not publish finalized terms or collect acceptance. The owner must approve the terms after legal review before presenting them as an active agreement.

Once approved terms are available, the guided setup should link to them and ask exactly:

> Before we get started, please review J-Stack’s Terms of Use. J-Stack is a free educational resource, and you decide which guidance to follow. Do you agree to the terms and want to continue?

Wait for an affirmative answer before continuing that guided setup. Silence, opening a link, or connecting an account is not an affirmative answer. If the user declines or is unsure, pause and offer to explain the terms or leave them to browse the public catalog.

Terms consent does **not** authorize account connections, sending questions, execution, spending, or monthly scheduling. Ask for those separately when relevant. Do not add public consent records or telemetry to this repository.

## 3. Find a useful starting point

Tell your assistant a goal, the project or situation it relates to, and any important constraints such as time, budget, or tools you already use. It should inspect the full catalog before narrowing its recommendations—not assume that the newest item is the best fit.

| Browse | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| [Personal Workflows](personal-workflows/README.md) | Guidance organized around personal outcomes. |
| [Business Workflows](business-workflows/README.md) | Guidance and skills organized around business outcomes. |
| [Libraries](libraries/README.md) | Reusable tools, UI resources, references, and guides. |
| [Insights](insights/README.md) | Lessons, thoughts, observations, and news context. |
| [Updates](updates/README.md) | Jake’s monthly record of what has been added or changed. |

The assistant should read promising resources closely, explain why a few match your goal, and note anything that needs checking. If nothing fits, it should say so rather than force a recommendation or invent a workflow. You can choose one item and ask for a walkthrough, with each action considered separately.

A simple starting request is:

> Read J-Stack as a guide. Ask about my goals if you need more context, find a few relevant resources across the catalog, and explain where I could start. Do not install, connect, send, schedule, or change anything.

## 4. Choose whether you want monthly suggestions

You can request a [monthly relevance check](libraries/guides/monthly-relevance-check.md) whenever you like. It combines **Your J-Stack update**—Jake’s undelivered written entries, kept intact—with **Ideas for you**, a few explained matches from the entire catalog.

Recurring check-ins are optional. If your host has a scheduler, your assistant can offer to help choose a day, time, and timezone, explain the scope and any private delivery-state storage, and ask for your consent before creating anything. Terms consent and account connections do not opt you in. If the host cannot schedule, use an on-demand check instead; do not claim a reminder was created.

You should be able to change or stop a recurring check through that host’s controls. This repository does not implement a scheduler, account monitoring, delivery tracking service, or telemetry.

## 5. Ask a question

If the guide does not answer something, your assistant can help prepare a concise question for Jake. **Questions are brought to Jake for consideration, and selected answers are shared in future updates.** Individual replies are not guaranteed.

The dedicated questions inbox is **not yet configured or verified**. Do not send to a guessed address. The [questions guide](libraries/guides/questions.md) explains how to prepare a private draft now and how a verified address could be used later, with your review and explicit permission to send.
